# Customer Communication

How to say it: approvals, denials, partials, delays, escalations, and tough moments. The goal is a customer who understands the outcome even when it is not the one they wanted.

## 1. Voice and principles

- **Warm, plain, specific.** Short sentences. One idea per sentence. No policy jargon or rule IDs in customer text.
- **Lead with the outcome,** then the why, then what to do next.
- **Own the merchant's mistakes** without defensiveness. Apologize once, for the real problem, then fix it.
- **Never blame or accuse.** Policies explain; they do not scold.
- **Be honest about limits** and timelines. Under-promise, then deliver early.
- **Give choices** when more than one remedy is available.

## 2. Message anatomy

1. Acknowledge (one line, specific to their situation)
2. Outcome (approved / partially approved / not eligible / needs review)
3. Breakdown or reason (numbers or the exact rule in plain words)
4. Next steps (what they do, what we do, by when)
5. Support route (how to reach us, how to appeal)

## 3. Templates

Replace {placeholders}. Keep the structure; adjust tone to the customer's.

**Approved: full refund**
> Thanks for letting us know, {first_name}. I'm sorry the {item} didn't work out. Your return is approved.
> Refund: {total} to your {payment_method} (item {items}, tax {tax}{, shipping {shipping}}). It's issued today and usually shows up in {eta}.
> Next: {return instructions: print label / drop-off / pickup}. Your RMA is {rma}. {Return shipping is on us.}
> If anything looks off, reply here and I'll fix it.

**Approved: partial (restocking fee / some items)**
> Good news, {first_name}: I can approve the return for {eligible_items}. 
> Because the {item} was opened, a {rate}% restocking fee of {fee} applies, so your refund is {total} to your {payment_method} (item {items}, fee -{fee}, tax {tax}).
> {Items not covered: {ineligible_item} is outside our {n}-day window / is final sale, so I can't refund it, but {alternative}.}
> Want me to go ahead? I'll start it as soon as you confirm.

**Not eligible: window passed**
> Thanks for reaching out, {first_name}. I checked your order and the {item} was delivered {n} days ago, and our window for {category} is {window} days, so I can't refund it as a change-of-mind return.
> Here's what I can do instead: {options: exchange / store credit / repair if it's faulty / manufacturer warranty}. If the item has a fault, tell me what's happening and I'll look at it under our defect cover, which lasts {defect_window} days.

**Not eligible: final sale / non-returnable**
> I'm sorry, {first_name}: {item} was marked {final sale / personalized / gift card} at checkout, which means we can't take it back for a change of mind. If it arrived damaged, defective or not as described, that's different and I'll take care of it right away, so please share a photo and a short description.

**Needs more information (fault claim)**
> I'm sorry about the {problem}, {first_name}. To fix this fast, could you send a clear photo of {what} (and the shipping label if it arrived damaged)? Once I have it I'll confirm your refund or replacement straight away.

**Escalated for review**
> Thanks, {first_name}. Because of the {value/situation}, this refund needs a quick review by a specialist. You don't need to send anything else. You'll hear from us within {SLA}. Your reference is {case_id}.

**Delay / status update**
> Quick update on {case_id}: {status}. We expect to {next action} by {date}. I'm sorry for the wait, and I'll keep you posted if anything changes.

**Refund issued**
> Your refund of {total} has been issued to your {payment_method} on {date}. Your bank can take {eta} to show it. If it hasn't appeared by {date + eta}, reply here and we'll trace it with the payment provider.

**Offer: store credit as an option (never a condition)**
> If you'd prefer, I can give you {amount} in store credit instantly instead of waiting for the refund to your {payment_method}. Your choice: either is fine.

**Verification needed**
> To protect your account, I need to verify a couple of details before I can look at this order. Could you confirm {email on order} and {order postcode / last four digits of the card}?

**Appeal outcome: upheld**
> I've reviewed your case again with fresh eyes, {first_name}. I understand this isn't the answer you hoped for. {Reason in one sentence.} {Remaining options.} If you'd like to take it further, {route}.

**Appeal outcome: overturned**
> Thank you for your patience, {first_name}. After a second review, I'm happy to say we're approving your {refund / exception}. {Details.} I'm sorry for the extra back-and-forth.

**Agent-to-agent/internal handoff note** — use the review packet in `escalation-and-approvals.md`.

## 4. Money formatting and clarity

- Always show **currency** and two decimals (or the currency's minor unit): `USD 357.10` or `$357.10`.
- Show a short breakdown for anything not a plain full refund: items, fee, tax, shipping, total.
- State the **method** and the **timing**: "to your Visa ending 4242, usually 5-7 business days." Never show full card numbers.
- Distinguish **issued by us** from **appears at your bank**.
- Mention who pays return shipping.

## 5. Denial playbook

1. Start with empathy tied to their situation, not boilerplate.
2. State the decision in the first two sentences.
3. Give the specific reason in plain words (window, condition, final sale).
4. Offer the best alternative(s) available **today**.
5. Explain how to appeal and who reviews.
6. Keep the door open: "If I've misunderstood anything, tell me and I'll recheck."

Check: would the customer be able to repeat the reason back accurately? If not, simplify.

## 6. De-escalation

For frustrated or angry customers:
- Name the feeling once ("I can see how frustrating this is"), then move to action.
- Avoid "calm down," "as I already said," "policy states" as an opener.
- Offer a concrete next step and a time.
- Stay consistent: do not trade the policy for relief from pressure.
- If the customer insists on a manager or regulator, comply gracefully and escalate with the packet.
- If abusive: one clear boundary ("I want to help, and I need us to keep this respectful"), then escalate or follow the abuse procedure. For threats of self-harm or violence, follow the crisis protocol, not the refund flow.

For customers in hardship or distress (bereavement, job loss, medical): prioritize speed and kindness, bring in a human, and use vulnerable-customer discretion.

## 7. Channel adaptation

| Channel | Style |
|---|---|
| Chat/WhatsApp | 1-3 short messages, one question at a time, emojis only if the customer uses them |
| Email | Structured: outcome, breakdown, next steps, signature; subject lines with order ID and outcome |
| Voice | Say the amount slowly, confirm back, send a written summary |
| Social media | Acknowledge publicly, move details to private message, never post personal data |
| In-app | Cards with breakdown and a single clear action button |

## 8. Words to avoid

- "Unfortunately" as a constant opener; "per our policy" with nothing else; "you should have..."; "as stated in the terms"; "we cannot do anything"; "that's not our fault"; "calm down"; "fraud/suspicious/abuse"; "bot cannot".
- Over-promising: "guaranteed," "definitely by tomorrow" unless true.

Prefer: "here's what I can do," "I checked," "what I can offer is," "I'd be glad to look at that."

## 9. Accessibility and localization

- Reading level around grade 7-8; avoid idioms.
- Use the customer's language where supported; do not machine-translate legal text without review; keep numbers, dates and currency in local format.
- Screen-reader friendly: simple lists, descriptive links, no image-only content.
- Offer a phone or human route for customers who cannot use the digital flow.
